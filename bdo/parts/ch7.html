Chapter VII THE GUIDE

Finding the Exact Family Tree

Ինչպես վերականգնել տոհմածառը

The full tree from Bdo to today — the Armenian tohmatsar (տոհմածառ) — can be rebuilt. The method is to start with the living and work backwards, one generation and one record at a time.

Each period of the family’s history left a different kind of record, kept in a different place. Think of the search as five layers, from the present back to Bdo:

Today → 1930s
Living memory

Elders, family papers, photos, letters, gravestones. Quickest and most important.

1991 → 1920s
Soviet civil records

Birth, marriage and death certificates in Georgia and Armenia.

1920s → 1830s
Church registers

Baptisms, marriages and burials at St Hakob, Pokr Khanchali.

1897 → 1830s
Imperial lists

Household lists and censuses naming each man and his father.

Before 1830
Karin

The hardest layer. Few Ottoman records name villagers.

Step by step

  1. Begin with the eldersRecord (audio or video) the oldest Bdoyans, especially anyone born in Pokr Khanchali before about 1960. Ask for names, nicknames, the branch or “house” within the family, where each brother went, and who married whom. Copy every document and photograph, and write down who is in each picture. Where: every Bdoyan household. Share the results, because each branch remembers a different part.
  2. Read the stonesPhotograph every Bdoyan gravestone in the cemetery around St Hakob in Pokr Khanchali, and in Ninotsminda and Akhalkalaki. Note names, dates and any patronymic (“son of …”). Gravestones often fill gaps for the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Where: Pokr Khanchali church cemetery; town cemeteries of Javakhk.
  3. Search every spellingThe name appears as Բդոյան in Armenian, Бдоян in Russian and ბდოიანი (Bdoiani) in Georgian records, and as Bdoyan, Bdojan or Bdoian in Latin script. Older documents may write the clan form, “of the house of Bdo”.
    Collect civil recordsFor people born or married after the 1920s, request certified extracts of birth, marriage and death records. Close relatives can usually request them; for older entries the archive of the civil registry may need to be searched. Georgia: Public Service Development Agency (civil registry, Ministry of Justice). Armenia: Civil Status Registration Agency (ՔԿԱԳ), Ministry of Justice.
  4. Reading a registerEntries are in Armenian, later partly in Russian. A baptism gives the child’s name, the dates of birth and baptism, and both parents’ names; a marriage names the fathers of bride and groom. Each entry lets you climb one generation.
    Search the church registers (metrical books)From the 1830s until about 1920, the priests of St Hakob kept registers of baptisms, marriages and deaths. Copies went to the diocesan consistory in Tiflis. Each entry names the parents, so you can climb back a generation at a time. Start with: FamilySearch films 1915055 and 1915180 (see the box above). Then: National Archives of Armenia, Yerevan (archives.am), for the original files; and the Central Historical Archive in Tbilisi (archive.gov.ge), whose online finder lists about 400 metrical books from 177 Armenian churches in Georgia. Ask for “Pokr (Patara) Khanchali, Akhalkalaki uyezd” — Tiflis governorate from 1867, Kutais governorate before that.
  5. Ask for these, in Russianметрические книги — metrical (church) books
    посемейные списки — family lists, 1886
    камеральные описания — household descriptions
    перепись 1897 года — 1897 census
    Find the first Russian listsThe household lists and descriptions drawn up in the 1830s–1880s, the family lists of 1886 and the 1897 census list every household head with his sons and their ages. The earliest of these, made soon after 1830, are the most likely documents to name Bdo’s sons or grandsons with their fathers’ names — the bridge back to Bdo himself. Where: National Archives of Georgia (Tbilisi), where a professional archive researcher can locate and photograph the Khanchali pages. Online now: the printed 1887 summary of the 1886 family lists for the Akhalkalaki district, with totals for every village, is scanned by the Russian State Public Historical Library (elib.shpl.ru) and the Presidential Library (prlib.ru).
  6. Read Vard Bdoyan’s book, then look for his notebooksHis 2014 book is free online. Its notes on storytellers (pp. 707–738) give names, birth years, parents and marriages, and pp. 148–152 describe the village house by house. Every note cites one of his field notebooks (“personal archive no. 79”, and so on): ask his descendants, the book’s editor and the Institute where those notebooks are now. Read: The Ethnography and Folk Art of the Armenians of Javakhk (YSU Press, 2014), arar.sci.am. Ask: Institute of Archaeology and Ethnography, 15 Charents St, Yerevan (iae.am); Yerevan State University Press; the History Museum of Armenia; the Akhalkalaki regional museum.
  7. Which test?Y-DNA (men only) follows the unbroken father-to-son line and can prove descent from Bdo.
    Autosomal (anyone) finds cousins on all sides, back about five or six generations.
    Test DNAAsk two or three Bdoyan men from different branches — one of them from the Abul line — to take a Y-DNA test (Y-37 or higher), and join the Armenian DNA Project. Matching results confirm a shared male-line ancestor. Autosomal tests (such as AncestryDNA, MyHeritage or FamilyTreeDNA Family Finder) help connect cousins through female lines. Where: FamilyTreeDNA, Armenian DNA Project (familytreedna.com).
  8. Build and share one treePut everything into a single shared online tree, cite the source for every fact, and invite each branch to add its own line. Where: FamilySearch (free, familysearch.org), Geni or MyHeritage. On FamilySearch, also search the indexed collection Armenia, Church Books, 1838–1929 (about 94,000 names) for Բդոյան, Бдоян and Bdoyan.

Questions to ask the elders

Background on KarinFor the villages of the Karin plain before 1915, see the Houshamadyan project (houshamadyan.org), which documents the Armenian towns and villages of the Ottoman Empire. Ottoman records from before 1830 rarely name individual villagers, so the earliest Russian lists are the realistic bridge to Bdo.
  • What was your father’s name, and his father’s, as far back as you know? (The old ideal was yot port — seven generations.)
  • Which branch are we — which of the five houses in Pokr Khanchali (Harutyun’s, Grigor’s, Anton and Davit’s, Mesrop’s, Zakar’s), and who lived there with whom?
  • What is remembered of Bdo’s full name, of Bdoyents Papo and his mill, and of the Bdoyans of Abul?
  • Which brothers or cousins left, when, and where did they go?
  • Who married into the family, and from which villages and families?
  • Are there documents, letters, Bibles or photographs with names and dates written on them?
