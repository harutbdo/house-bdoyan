Chapter V THE BLOODLINE

One Ancestor, One Family

Մեկ նախահայր, մեկ ընտանիք
THE CORE OF THE FAMILY STORY

Every Bdoyan is a descendant of Bdo. The name itself is the record: Bdoyan means “of Bdo”.

Բոլոր Բդոյանները Բդոյի ժառանգներն են։

Common surnames like Grigoryan or Hakobyan arose independently in hundreds of places, from hundreds of unrelated men named Grigor or Hakob. Bdoyan is different.

  1. The name points to one man.Bdo was a nickname, most likely for Petros. Countless men named Petros left sons who became Petrosyans; only in one known household did the nickname itself harden into the surname. Bdoyan is the name of one house, not a common pattern.
  2. One village, one house.The church archives confirm that Pokr Khanchali’s families came from Dinarigom on the Karin plain, where, the family remembers, “Bdo’s people” were a recognised house.
  3. They moved together and stayed together.The family crossed the frontier as a group in 1829–1830 and put down roots in one village, Pokr Khanchali. There its patriarch, Bdoyents Papo, built the first mill, and eighty years later the Bdoyans still lived wall to wall as one kin quarter.
  4. The name came from Karin.Clans that moved to Javakhk kept the names they had borne in Karin before 1829. Bdoyans are few in number, and as far as is known, all trace their roots to Javakhk — and through it to the Karin plain.

So the Bdoyans are not simply people who share a name. They are one extended family, cousins of one another at greater or lesser distance, all descended in the male line from Bdo. His blood also runs in many families with other surnames, through the daughters of each generation who married into other houses.

Proving it with DNABecause a Y-chromosome passes almost unchanged from father to son, Bdoyan men from different branches should share Bdo’s Y-DNA signature. The most telling test now is to compare a man of the Abul line with one from Pokr Khanchali: a match would join the two branches scientifically and would show Bdo’s deep ancestral lineage in the Armenian Highlands.

The Bdoyans of Abul

Vard’s notes also reveal a second Bdoyan line in Javakhk. In Abul, a village 10 km east of Akhalkalaki, lived the singer and church clerk Smbat Bdoyan (1887–1963), whose grandfather Hakob Bdoyan had come to Abul in 1830 from Umudum, a Karin village 16 km north of the city — almost exactly as far out as Dinarigom. The Abul singers Ruben and Sirun Bdoyan, whom Vard recorded in Yerevan in 1965, still sang songs that had come with their family from Umudum.

The family’s account and this record point the same way: back to one small stretch of the Karin plain north of the city. A great household there could easily have had a branch in a neighbouring village before 1829, through a son who took land or a brother who married into it, and in the family’s view — that every Bdoyan descends from Bdo — Abul’s Hakob was of Bdo’s line too. This is the one link in the family story that a document or a DNA test still has to confirm, and Chapter VII shows how.
