This is the story of one household that became a family name. It begins with a man called Bdo (Bedros) on the high plain of Karin, follows his descendants across a closing frontier in 1829–1830 to Lake Khanchali, and gathers every Bdoyan the records name, from his time to ours.

AT A GLANCE
  • c. 1740–1780Bdo (Bedros) born; head of a household on the Karin plain (estimate)
  • 1828–1829Russo-Turkish war; Russian army takes Kars, Akhalkalaki, Akhaltsikhe and, in 1829, Erzurum
  • 14 Sept 1829Treaty of Adrianople hands Karin back to the Ottomans
  • 1829–1830Mass exodus from Karin; Bdo’s descendants help settle Pokr Khanchali
  • 1830sBdoyents Papo, patriarch of the village’s Bdoyans, builds its first mill
  • 1850s · 1865 · 1875Village church first recorded; parish school at work; St Hakob rebuilt in stone
  • 4 March 1910Vard Harutyuni Bdoyan born in Pokr Khanchali
  • 1938–1986Vard Bdoyan’s career as an ethnographer in Yerevan; his Javakhk book appears in 2014
  • 20th–21st c.Branches in Yerevan, Kharkiv, the Rostov region and beyond; about 1,440 Bdoyans today
