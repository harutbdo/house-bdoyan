Chapter VI THE FAMILY

The Bdoyans We Know

Մեզ հայտնի Բդոյանները

Two centuries of the family can now be pinned to real people. This chapter gathers every Bdoyan named in the sources, shows how they connect, and marks how sure each link is.

Most of these names come from one place: the notes Vard Bdoyan wrote about the people who told him stories and sang him songs between 1938 and 1980, and the plan of Pokr Khanchali he drew in 1950.Bdoyan, Ethnography and Folk Art of Javakhk, 8, 150–152, 683, 707–738. Together they give three groups: the Bdoyans of Pokr Khanchali, a branch in the town of Akhalkalaki, and the Bdoyans of Abul, whose ancestor came from Umudum.

What we still don’t know

The chart has four gaps that matter more than any others. Each can be closed with the records listed in the research handbook.

  1. Bdo’s baptismal name.Bedros (Petros) is probable but unproven. The earliest burial and baptism registers of Pokr Khanchali, and the first Russian household lists of the 1830s, may name him or his sons.
  2. Who Papo was.“Papo” is a pet name too. Was he Bdo’s son or his grandson, and what was his full name?
  3. The two missing generations.Between Papo and the five householders of the 1910s lie two generations with no names yet. The parish registers of the 1840s–1880s should supply them; the first step can be the record of Harutyun’s marriage in 1887, which should name his father.
  4. The Abul link.Were Hakob of Umudum and the Bdoyans of Dinarigom one family before 1829? A Y-DNA comparison between the two lines would answer it fastest.
